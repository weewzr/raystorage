Process route of the Base Case (Sheets 12 to 17, PDF p35 to p40; PFD PDF p42). Natural gas arrives at 9 C and 7.0 MPa, is preheated to 135 C and let down to 3.70 MPa, and splits into feedstock (stream 2) and burner fuel (stream 3). The feedstock takes a small H2 recycle, is desulphurised, mixed with HP steam (overall steam to carbon 2.7 to 2.8), pre-reformed adiabatically, given a second steam addition and reformed in the terrace-wall tubes (3.3 to 4 % CH4 dry at the outlet). The effluent is cooled to 320 C in the reformer waste heat boiler, shifted in one HT shift reactor, cooled, stripped of condensate and sent to the PSA. The PSA gives 40 C, 2.5 MPa hydrogen; its tail gas is the primary burner fuel and is topped up with natural gas. Combustion air is preheated by the flue gas, which leaves at 136 C. Excess HP steam (4.23 MPa, 395 C, stream 10) drives a condensing turbine for 11.5 MWe gross.

### 7.2 Water cross-check and a discrepancy in stream 14

Net water consumed by the reactions = steam fed - condensate removed - water in PSA feed = (4,029.1 + 155.2) - 1,772.9 - 15.8 = 2,395.5 kmol/h = 43.2 t/h (calculated). The utilities sheet (PDF p48) lists losses of 44.2 t/h for the hydrogen plant 'including water consumed in the reaction and deaerator vent'. The difference of 1.0 t/h is of the size of a deaerator vent (not itemised). Stream 14 'HP steam to process' is 95,301 kg/h (5290.1 kmol/h). If it were all reformer steam, the second addition would be 1261 kmol/h instead of 155, S/C would be 3.35, and net water consumption would be 63.1 t/h, which is inconsistent with the 44.2 t/h. Conclusion: the H and O balances imply about 75.4 t/h of reformer steam, and with that flow the net water consumption matches the utilities sheet; stream 14 is about 19.9 t/h above it and the report does not say where the difference goes (unresolved). No carbon balance depends on stream 14.

## 8. Does the published 0.8091 kg/Nm3 H2 include the tail-gas combustion and the process CO2?

**Yes, all of it.** The Base Case has no capture and one carbon outlet (flue gas, stream 9). Evidence in order of strength:

- Stream 9 carries 1,838.4 kmol/h CO2 = 80.906 t/h = 0.80906 kg/Nm3 H2, which reproduces the Table 2 value (-0.005 %). The flue gas is what is left after burning the tail gas and the supplementary NG, so the figure includes both.
- The carbon chain closes from NG to stack (-0.085 %), with the tail gas (stream 8) accounting for 85.8 % and the supplementary NG (stream 3) for 14.2 % of the carbon reaching the stack (calculated). No carbon is credited anywhere else.
- The report states (executive summary, PDF p6) that 'all of the CO2 is emitted from the flue gas of the steam reformer' and that it is produced during reforming and shift and during the combustion of the residual CO in the PSA tail gas and of the supplementary natural gas. Tail gas is 'the primary fuel' of the burners (same page; Sheet 14, PDF p37).
- Table 2 treats 'Specific CO2 Emissions' as the stack emission (the Case 1A and 2A capture rates reproduce from it, section 7.8). The Base Case value equals the stack CO2 within 0.0053 %, so no credit for the 9.918 MWe exported and no indirect term is included.
- Corroboration from Case 2A, which captures CO2 from the same PSA tail gas: the Case 2A equipment list (PDF p152, sheet 4 of 5) gives a tail-gas compressor flow of 47,204 Nm3/h, MW 28.8 and 28 C, against 47,211 Nm3/h (-0.014 %) and 28.80 kg/kmol from Base Case stream 8 (calculated), so the tail gas is the same and the 22.414 Nm3/kmol convention holds. The Case 2A text (PDF p136, Sheet 50 of 112) says the tail gas holds about 51 mol % CO2 and the washed gas 3.5 mol %, which removes about 96.5 % of its CO2 (calculated, wet basis, indicative). Table 2 gives 0.4556 kg/Nm3 captured in Case 2A, which is 96.5 % of the pass-through CO2 of Base Case stream 8 (0.4723 kg/Nm3, calculated). The tail-gas capture plant therefore removes CO2 that the Base Case counts inside the 0.8091.

Feedstock carbon in stream 2 is 1,578.8 kmol C/h (69.48 t CO2 equivalent), which equals the carbon in stream 8 (1,578.5) within -0.022 %; 29.1 kmol/h of it is CO2 already present in the NG feed (2 vol %).

**Rule for later model work, so nothing is counted twice.** The stack CO2 of the Base Case is A + B = 0.8091 kg/Nm3 H2 (8.996 kg/kg H2). Row A (0.694 kg/Nm3, rescaled) is the tail-gas term and already contains its own combustion. If a model burns the tail gas explicitly, it must take its carbon from the tail-gas composition (A1 + A2 + A3) and add only the supplementary NG (B) on top; it must not add a tail-gas combustion term to the published 0.8091. If a model starts from 0.8091, only changes relative to the Base Case (a different tail-gas composition, a different supplementary NG flow) may be added. A capture or abatement model that acts on the tail gas can remove at most A (0.694 kg/Nm3, 85.8 % of the stack CO2); one that acts on the flue gas sees all of A + B.

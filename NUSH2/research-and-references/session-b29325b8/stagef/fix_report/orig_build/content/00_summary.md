# Executive Summary

Singapore imports all of its natural gas, and conventional steam methane reforming (SMR) emits CO₂ from both its furnace and its reforming chemistry. NUS-H₂ removes the furnace. A {{R_RATED_MWTH|f0|u}} high-temperature gas-cooled reactor (HTGR) heats the reformer through helium, and 30~[[wt%]] monoethanolamine (MEA) captures CO₂ from shifted syngas. The plant is a new build at SOXAL scale on the proposed Western Island, a siting proposal of this study with no official approval. It makes {{H2_B_NM3H|u}} of purified hydrogen, {{H2_B_T_Y|u:tH2/y}} at {{AVAILABILITY_PCT|f0}}% availability, from {{Q_PROCESS_B_MWTH|u}} of process heat and a reactor need of {{R_NEEDED_B_MWTH|u}}. Pressure swing adsorption (PSA) stays an external block, and its reject returns to the reformer as a stream crossing the boundary.

Net abatement is {{A_ATM_B_MT|u:MtCO2e/y}} against a same-size unabated SMR, which meets the {{TARGET_ABATEMENT_MT|f2|u:MtCO2e/y}} target by a factor of {{ABATEMENT_MULTIPLE}}. The {{TARGET_COST_S_T|f0|u}} cost target is missed in all {{TARGET_COST_N_CENTRAL}} central cases. The base full cost is {{AC_L5_BOAK_25Y|u}} at 25~[[y]] and {{AC_L5_BOAK_60Y|u}} at 60~[[y]] for a build between first and Nth of a kind (BOAK), and {{AC_L5_FOAK_25Y|u}} and {{AC_L5_FOAK_60Y|u}} for a first of a kind (FOAK). A discounted cash flow (DCF) reproduces these within {{DCF_TEST_MAX_GAP_PCT}}%. The levelised cost of hydrogen is {{LCOH_NUSH2_BOAK_25Y|u}} at BOAK, 25~[[y]], against {{LCOH_SMR_25Y|u}} for the unabated SMR.

A named scenario, MATURE_60Y, states what it would take to meet the target. A tenth-of-a-kind reactor over 60~[[y]] with gas at {{STEPDOWN_4_GAS_S_GJ|u}}, against the central {{STEPDOWN_1_GAS_S_GJ|f0|u}}, gives {{STEPDOWN_4_L5|u}} on the ledger and {{STEPDOWN_4_DCF|u}} by DCF. Dearer gas lowers the abatement cost because NUS-H₂ burns less gas than the unabated plant. Deployment towards 2050 depends on a qualified reactor and intermediate heat exchanger (IHX), a design-specific emergency planning zone (EPZ), a licensing framework and a contracted CO₂ storage pathway.

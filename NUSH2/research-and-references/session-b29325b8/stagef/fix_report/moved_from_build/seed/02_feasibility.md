2. Feasibility and benefits

The proposed system combines established SMR and CO₂-capture operations with the continuous high-temperature heat supplied by an HTGR. Japan’s High Temperature Engineering Test Reactor achieved a 950 [[degC]] reactor outlet temperature [@r11], and China’s High-Temperature Gas-cooled Reactor Pebble-bed Module (HTR-PM) entered commercial operation in December 2023 [@r12]. These developments support the technical basis for helium-cooled reactors. The proposed Singapore integration applies the INL process conditions to an industrial hydrogen train on Jurong Island.

2.1 Reactor safety and emergency planning

Tristructural isotropic (TRISO) fuel particles incorporate multiple protective layers that retain fission products, while helium cooling and the graphite-based core support high-temperature operation [@r9]. The primary helium boundary, IHX and independent secondary helium loop provide successive physical barriers between the reactor and hydrogen-production equipment. Monitoring and isolation at these interfaces support plant protection and reliable process-heat delivery.

Singapore’s compact geography makes site-specific emergency planning essential [@r13]. Advanced-reactor emergency-planning assessments use accident frequencies and projected radiological consequences to determine protective actions [@r14,r15,r16]. Figure 3 illustrates this frequency-consequence method using the Xe-100 safety-case example [@r17].

The Xe-100 assessment presents a 400 m emergency-planning reference distance for its specific safety case [@r17,r18]. HTR-PM studies also evaluate offsite emergency-planning arrangements [@r19,r20]. These studies provide relevant methodologies for assessing a future Singapore HTGR site. The appropriate emergency arrangements will be established through a reactor-specific safety case and regulatory review.

2.2 Advantages of HTGR-assisted process heat

The proposed 230 [[MWth]] HTGR supplies the INL reference reforming duty of 176.8 [[MWth]] through the IHX and secondary helium circuit [@r8]. Helium heats the reformer directly, so no natural gas is burned for reforming heat, and the chemical plant retains established reforming, WGS and amine-capture equipment. A simplified allocation of reactor heat to auxiliary electricity gives a combined screening requirement of 220.4 [[MWth]] (Appendix A). The remaining 9.6 [[MWth]] provides preliminary design flexibility. Integrated heat recovery and the MEA regeneration duty will determine the final steam and electricity balance. The assessment applies the INL 130 MMSCFD hydrogen-production basis throughout.

2.3 Regulation and community engagement

Singapore is strengthening its capability to assess advanced nuclear technologies. A Phase 1 International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) Integrated Nuclear Infrastructure Review (INIR) is planned for 2027 [@r21,r22]. The review addresses 19 areas of nuclear infrastructure, including safety, emergency preparedness and radioactive waste management. This programme builds the national capability needed to assess industrial nuclear process-heat applications.

The Singapore Nuclear Research and Safety Institute contributes reactor modelling, safety analysis, technical expertise and public education [@r22,r23,r24]. These capabilities support the evaluation of HTGR technology, high-temperature heat transfer and industrial integration.

The National Environment Agency’s nuclear and radiation safety responsibilities, international regulatory cooperation and the Energy Market Authority’s advanced-nuclear feasibility study strengthen Singapore’s institutional basis [@r25,r26,r27]. A future project requires a coordinated licensing framework covering reactor operation, the nuclear-industrial heat interface and emergency preparedness.

Early engagement with Jurong Island operators, workers and neighbouring stakeholders will support informed assessment of the proposed facility [@r23,r24,r28]. Clear communication of its industrial purpose, protective design features and projected emissions benefits will be central to project development.

2.4 Risks, constraints and limitations

The assessment adopts INL process conditions [@r8,r10], a proposed 230 [[MWth]] reactor rating and a screening-level MEA capture assumption. The quantitative boundary covers nuclear process heat, SMR, WGS and MEA capture; downstream PSA and external tail-gas handling are excluded in accordance with the agreed study scope, and their emissions remain outside the reported totals. The 39.7% power-conversion assumption, IHX performance and MEA regeneration heat recovery require integrated verification. Chapters 3 and 4 evaluate upstream emissions and economic parameters through documented assumptions and sensitivity analysis. Table 1 summarises the principal project constraints.

2.5 Proposed plant scale and implementation

Jurong Island provides a conceptual deployment location with established energy and chemical infrastructure, logistics and potential hydrogen consumers [@r29]. The reference scenario comprises one proposed 230 [[MWth]] HTGR serving a single SMR + 30 wt% MEA CCS train at the INL production benchmark of 130 MMSCFD hydrogen, approximately 97,946 [[tH2/y]] at 85% availability. Captured CO₂ would be conditioned for transport and geological storage through a future cross-border pathway supported by Singapore-Indonesia CCS cooperation [@r30]. The environmental and economic comparisons use this single production basis.

2.5.1 Indicative implementation roadmap

The indicative roadmap targets potential operation by 2050 (Table 2). National nuclear capability development and overseas reactor and IHX qualification precede Singapore-specific site assessment, licensing, investment and construction. Each phase advances once the corresponding technical, regulatory and commercial requirements are satisfied. Relevant stakeholders include the Energy Market Authority, National Environment Agency, Singapore Nuclear Research and Safety Institute, Singapore Economic Development Board, reactor developers, Jurong Island operators, hydrogen buyers and CO₂ storage partners.

The roadmap builds on Singapore’s planned 2027 INIR review and advances high-temperature heat-transfer qualification, site assessment, licensing and commercial contracting. The 2050 horizon provides a long-term planning target, and the approximately 11 years between HTR-PM construction start and commercial operation provide one historical reference for the construction and commissioning schedule [@r12].

The Future Energy Fund and other potential industrial partnerships could provide a policy context for supporting early demonstration and integration activities.

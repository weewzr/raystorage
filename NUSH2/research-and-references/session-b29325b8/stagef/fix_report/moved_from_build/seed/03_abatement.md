3. Net Emissions Abatement

3.1. Top Down Equations

The net environmental performance of the proposed HTGR-SMR plant integrated with CCS is evaluated by measuring the operational greenhouse gas emissions avoided relative to a conventional, unabated SMR benchmark delivering the identical quantity of hydrogen feedstock. Net annual emissions abatement (ΔEₙₑₜ) is defined as the difference between the conventional baseline operational emissions (Ebaseline) and the residual operational emissions generated by the proposed plant (Eresidual):

ΔEₙₑₜ = Ebaseline − Eresidual

Baseline emissions are determined from the sum of the dilute flue gas generated by furnace fuel combustion (Ecomb, baseline) and the concentrated carbon dioxide stream generated by the stoichiometric process syngas reactions (Eproc, baseline):

Ebaseline = Ecomb, baseline + Eproc, baseline

For the proposed facility, direct furnace combustion is completely eliminated (Ecomb, proposed = 0) because high-temperature nuclear helium delivers all endothermic reforming heat. Furthermore, auxiliary electrical imports from the local power network are zero (Eaux, grid = 0) because all parasitic mechanical, pumping, and compression loads are supplied via internal nuclear co-generation. Consequently, residual emissions consist solely of the uncaptured carbon dioxide slip exiting the syngas amine absorption unit (Eproc, slip):

Eresidual = Eproc, slip

All values are evaluated in metric tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent per year (tCO₂e/year) across a standardized 365-day operating year adjusted for plant availability.

3.2. System boundary, conventional comparison and assumptions

The assessment boundary applies a gate-to-gate accounting scope encompassing two integrated subsystems: an onshore nuclear island comprising a 210 [[MWth]] HTGR, primary and intermediate helium circuits, and auxiliary power turbomachinery; and a chemical plant consisting of natural gas desulfurisation, a helium-heated catalytic reformer, a water-gas shift reactor, an amine chemical absorption unit, a hydrogen pressure swing adsorption system, and a multi-stage carbon dioxide compression train.

Life cycle burdens associated with nuclear fuel fabrication, upstream plant construction, and downstream cross-border CO₂ maritime transport and storage are excluded from the primary ledger and evaluated under environmental sensitivity. Upstream natural gas extraction and pipeline transmission, downstream cross-border liquefied carbon dioxide shipping, subsea trunklines, and permanent sub-seabed geological sequestration are excluded from the facility boundary. Detailed pressure swing adsorption tail-gas processing is likewise excluded from direct boundary accounting.

The conventional baseline is established at the operational scale of Singapore Oxygen Air Liquide’s (SOXAL) SMR facility on Jurong Island, which has a nameplate throughput of 100,000 normal cubic metres per hour of hydrogen. Using standard hydrogen gas density (0.08988 kg/Nm³), this corresponds to an hourly production capacity of 8.988 t H₂/h and a daily throughput of 215.71 t H₂/day. Operating across an assumed 85% plant availability factor (7,446 equivalent full-load hours per year), total annual output equals 66,927 tonnes H₂/year.

Baseline emissions intensity is benchmarked against empirical facility-level data from 33 operational SMR facilities compiled by Cho et al. (2022), which establish a mean direct operational intensity of 9.35 kg CO₂e/kg H₂. Thermodynamic energy balances and reaction stoichiometry define a split emission profile: furnace combustion accounts for approximately 35% (3.27 tCO₂e/t H₂) to supply the 800–900 [[degC]] endothermic reaction heat, while stoichiometric process syngas conversion accounts for the remaining 65% (6.08 tCO₂e/t H₂). For the proposed architecture, an Intermediate Heat Exchanger transfers 184.9 [[MWth]] of secondary helium heat at 900 [[degC]] into the reformer to fully displace furnace firing, while 11.8 [[MWe]] of electricity co-generated from the remaining 25.1 [[MWth]] reactor balance covers all parasitic carbon capture and compression demands. Commercial chemical amine absorption applied to the process syngas stream is modelled at an industry-standard capture efficiency of 90% (Sheng, 2023).

3.3. Net Emissions Abated

Baseline operational emissions across the SOXAL annual throughput of 66,927 tonnes H₂/year equal 625,767 tCO₂e/year (0.626 MtCO₂e/year). This baseline total is composed of 218,851 tCO₂e/year emitted from the furnace flue gas and 406,916 tCO₂e/year generated within the chemical process syngas stream.

Under the proposed HTGR-SMR-CCS facility architecture, substituting furnace combustion with 900 [[degC]] helium process heat avoids 218,851 tCO₂e/year of combustion emissions entirely. The amine chemical absorption unit captures 90% of the concentrated syngas carbon dioxide, routing 366,224 tCO₂e/year into the multi-stage compression train for offshore storage conditioning. The remaining 10% uncaptured syngas fraction constitutes the plant's sole direct operational emission, resulting in a residual footprint of 40,692 tCO₂e/year (0.041 MtCO₂e/year). Because all parasitic auxiliary electricity loads (~11 [[MWe]]) are satisfied on-site by dedicated nuclear power co-generation, electrical import emissions from the domestic power grid are zero.

Subtracting the residual process slip from the unabated baseline yields a net annual emissions abatement of 585,075 tCO₂e/year (0.585 MtCO₂e/year). This outcome corresponds to an overall facility emissions reduction of 93.5% and decreases direct hydrogen production carbon intensity from 9.35 to 0.61 kg CO₂e/kg H₂. The net abatement of 0.585 MtCO₂e/year exceeds the project requirement of 0.25 MtCO₂e/year by more than double, representing roughly 1.2% to 1.3% of Singapore’s total projected 2035 national carbon budget of 45 to 50 MtCO₂e.

3.4. Sensitivity and environmental interpretation

The sensitivity of net emissions abatement was evaluated across variations in amine capture efficiency and plant operating availability. Holding the high-temperature nuclear thermal loop constant, varying the chemical absorption capture rate across 80%, 85%, and 95% yields net annual abatement of 0.544 MtCO₂e/year, 0.565 MtCO₂e/year, and 0.605 MtCO₂e/year, respectively. Even in an operational contingency scenario where carbon capture equipment is temporarily taken offline, eliminating furnace fuel combustion through nuclear process heat sustains an inherent baseline mitigation of 0.219 MtCO₂e/year, demonstrating that thermal substitution decouples a significant share of decarbonisation from downstream carbon logistics.

Evaluating plant availability between a conservative 70% factor (6,132 operating hours per year) and a baseload 90% factor (7,884 operating hours per year) shifts net annual abatement within a range of 0.482 to 0.619 MtCO₂e/year, consistently surpassing the 0.25 MtCO₂e/year threshold. From an environmental perspective, this confirms that deploying an HTGR-SMR-CCS plant at the scale of Jurong Island’s existing hydrogen infrastructure delivers an inflexible, large-scale carbon abatement stream capable of insulating national industry from the grid-displacement penalties associated with fossil-fired electrification.

Expanding the accounting boundary beyond the plant gate to incorporate upstream nuclear lifecycle emissions (assuming ~5.5 kgCO₂e/MWh) and downstream offshore CO₂ transport and geological storage logistics (~0.025 tCO₂e/tCO₂ stored) imposes an incremental penalty of approximately 0.015 to 0.020 Mt CO₂e/year. Even with these lifecycle burdens deducted, net annual abatement remains robust at approximately 0.565 to 0.570 MtCO₂e/year, confirming that indirect lifecycle penalties do not alter the feasibility or compliance of the proposed plant against the >0.25 MtCO₂e/year threshold.

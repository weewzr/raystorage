Summary

Our report proposes integrating a 230 [[MWth]] high-temperature gas-cooled reactor (HTGR) with steam methane reforming (SMR) and 30 wt% monoethanolamine (MEA) carbon capture and storage (CCS) on Singapore’s Jurong Island. Nuclear-heated helium supplies the 176.8 [[MWth]] reforming duty of the Idaho National Laboratory (INL) reference plant, displacing natural gas combustion for reformer heat, while MEA absorption removes process CO₂ from shifted syngas. The design adopts the INL production basis of 130 MMSCFD of hydrogen, approximately 97,946 [[tH2/y]] at 85% availability. The quantitative boundary covers nuclear heat delivery, SMR, water-gas shift and MEA capture; downstream pressure swing adsorption (PSA) and external tail-gas handling lie outside it, so results are reported as in-scope operational abatement. In-scope operational abatement: [Chapter 3]. Incremental abatement cost: [Chapter 4]. Deployment towards 2050 requires qualified reactor and intermediate heat exchanger (IHX) technology, a design-specific emergency planning zone (EPZ), a nuclear licensing framework and a contracted CO₂ storage pathway.

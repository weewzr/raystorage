To use the High-Temperature Gas-cooled Reactor (HTGR) system as a commercial reactor similar to
the future HTGR ‘GTHTR300C,’ supplying stable high-temperature heat from the reactor to the heat
utilization system such as the hydrogen production system over a prolonged period should be demon-
strated. The 50-day high-temperature continuous operation with full power, which is the ﬁrst long-term
operation with a reactor outlet coolant temperature over 900C, was achieved in March 2010 in the High
Temperature Engineering Test Reactor (HTTR) of the Japan Atomic Energy Agency (JAEA). In order to
supply stable high-temperature heat from the reactor to the heat utilization system using the HTTR, the
heat exchange performance and structure integrity of the intermediate heat exchanger (IHX) should be
conﬁrmed. In this paper, the heat exchange performance and structure temperature of the IHX were
evaluated with the high-temperature continuous operation data and compared with the designed one or
estimated one. As a result, it was conﬁrmed that the IHX can exchange stable high-temperature heat from
the primary coolant to the secondary helium, and the IHX structure temperature is under the allowable
working temperature in the operation. Moreover, it was conﬁrmed that the heat exchange performance is
almost the same as the designed one and that the structure temperature estimated with the analysis code is
almost the same as the measured one.
KEYWORDS: HTGR, HTTR, high-temperature continuous operation, intermediate heat exchang-
er, heat exchange performance, structural integrity
I. Introduction
The
High
Temperature
Engineering
Test
Reactor
(HTTR),1) which is the ﬁrst High-Temperature Gas-cooled
Reactor (HTGR) in Japan, having a thermal power of
30 MW and 950C maximum reactor outlet coolant temper-
ature, was constructed in the Japan Atomic Energy Agency
(JAEA) for the purpose of establishing and improving the
technologies of HTGRs as well as nuclear heat utilization.
The HTTR attained its ﬁrst criticality in November 1998,
and in-service operation has been conducted through ﬁve-
phase rise-to-power tests.
To develop the commercial HTGR system such as the
future HTGR ‘GTHTR300C,’ supplying stable heat to a
heat utilization system over a prolonged period should be
demonstrated. Thus, the long-term continuous operation
with full power and over 900C reactor outlet coolant tem-
perature (called ‘high-temperature continuous operation’)
was planned.
Before the high-temperature continuous operation, the
long-term continuous operation with 850C maximum reac-
tor outlet coolant temperature mode was conducted from
March to April in 20072) in order to verify and conﬁrm the
safety of the high-temperature continuous operation.
The high-temperature continuous operation was started
from January 2010, and the 50-day high-temperature con-
tinuous operation with full power was achieved in March
2010.3) As a result of these operations, it was demonstrated
that the HTTR can supply stable heat to the heat utilization
system such as the thermochemical hydrogen production
system over a prolonged period. These operations are the
ﬁrst achievements in the history of the HTTR. The data
obtained, such as core burnup characteristics, helium purity
management, high-temperature instrument performance, and
core internal structure integrity, are very useful for the
development of a commercial HTGR.
The intermediate heat exchanger (IHX) is one of the high-
temperature instruments installed in the HTTR. It is planned
that the thermochemical hydrogen production system is con-
nected to the HTTR through the IHX, so the IHX is the most
important instrument in the development of a hydrogen
production system and the future HTGR ‘GTHTR300C.’

II. Outline of the HTTR and the High-Temperature
Continuous Operation
1. Outline of the HTTR
(1) Reactor and Fuel
Table 1 shows the major speciﬁcations of the HTTR and
Fig. 1 shows the structure of the reactor of the HTTR. The
reactor is composed of fuel elements, reﬂector block, core
restraint mechanism, reactor pressure vessel (RPV), and so
on. The reactor core is composed of 30 columns of the
fuel blocks and 7 columns of the control rod guide blocks.
The permanent reﬂector blocks and the replaceable reﬂector
blocks are arranged around the reactor core.
Figure 2 shows the components of the fuel element. The
fuel element of the HTTR is the so-called pin-in-block type
composed of fuel rods and a hexagonal graphite block (fuel
block). The fuel block has 31 or 33 holes, and the fuel rods
are inserted in the hole. The primary coolant ﬂows through
the annulus formed between the fuel rod and hole of the fuel
block.
In the reactor, the primary coolant ﬂows in from the
annulus of the primary concentric hot gas duct arranged at
the bottom of the RPV. It rises to the upper plenum through
the gap between the RPV and the permanent reﬂector blocks.
Next, it turns over and ﬂows downward through the fuel
channel. Lastly, heated coolant is joined at the hot plenum
and ﬂows out to the inner pipe of the primary concentric hot
gas duct through the primary coolant outlet nozzle.

cooling system is composed of the main cooling system
(MCS), the auxiliary cooling system (ACS), and the vessel
cooling system (VCS). The MCS is operated in normal
operation to remove the generated heat at the reactor.
The ACS and VCS are the engineered safety features. The
ACS is in standby during normal operation and starts up to
remove residual heat after a reactor scram. The VCS cools
the biological concrete shield surrounding the RPV at nor-

The MCS is composed of the primary cooling system, the
secondary cooling system, and the pressurized water cooling

HTTR has the following primary missions: (1) establish heat
utilization technology using heat generated at the reactor,
and (2) demonstrate the inherent safety features of HTGR.
To achieve mission (1), the 10 MW high-temperature gas-
gas-type heat exchanger is needed. Also, to achieve mission
(2), the 30 MW heat exchanger is needed. Thus, the heat
exchanger of one IHX and one primary pressurized water
cooler (PPWC) are installed in the primary cooling system
and connected to the reactor with the primary coolant con-
centric hot gas duct in the HTTR. The heat exchanger of one
secondary pressurized water cooler (SPWC) is installed in
the secondary cooling system and connected to the IHX with
the secondary helium concentric hot gas duct. The heat
exchanger of one air-cooler is installed in the pressurized
water cooling system. The primary gas circulators (PGCs)
are arranged around the IHX and PPWC; the secondary gas
circulator (SGC) is arranged around the SPWC. The heated
primary coolant at the reactor ﬂows inside the inner tube of
the primary concentric hot gas duct to the IHX and PPWC.
The primary coolant is cooled to about 400C at the IHX and
PPWC, and returns to the reactor through the annular space
between the inner pipe and outer pipe of the primary con-
centric hot gas duct.
The HTTR has two reactor outlet coolant temperature
modes and two systematical operation modes. One of the
reactor outlet coolant temperature modes is called the ‘‘Rated
operation mode’’ with a maximum reactor outlet coolant
temperature of 850C; the other is the ‘‘High-temperature
test operation mode’’ with a maximum reactor outlet coolant
temperature of 950C. In these operation modes, the reactor
outlet coolant temperature is changed by changing the pri-
mary coolant mass ﬂow rate.
The HTTR has two systematical operation modes of
‘‘Single-loaded operation mode’’ and ‘‘Parallel-loaded oper-
ation mode.’’ The generated heat of 30 MW at the reactor
is removed using only the PPWC in the single-loaded oper-
ation mode. On the other hand, the generated heat is re-
moved using both the PPWC and IHX in the parallel-loaded
operation mode; the amounts of heat removed are 20 MW at
the PPWC and 10 MW at the IHX. Thus, the HTTR oper-
ation mode is generally called the ‘‘Rated/Single-loaded
operation.’’

The hot primary coolant enters into the IHX through the
inner tube of the primary concentric hot gas duct. It is
deﬂected under the hot header and discharged around the
heat transfer tubes to transfer the heat to the secondary
helium cooling system. It ﬂows to the PGC via the upper
outlet nozzle and ﬂows back to the annular space between
the inner shell and outer shell.
On the other hand, the secondary helium gas ﬂows down-
wards in the heat transfer tubes and upwards in the central
hot gas duct through the hot header. The inner thermal
insulator is installed inside the inner shell to maintain its
temperature under the allowable one. The thermal insulators
are installed outside and inside of the central hot gas duct.
The heat transfer is reduced by them at the central hot gas
duct so that high eﬃciency can be obtained. Moreover, the
central hot gas duct temperature is kept under the allowable
working temperature by them. The pressure of the secondary
helium is controlled higher than that in the primary coolant
because of the prevention of the primary helium leak due to
the rupture of the heat transfer tube in the IHX.
The tube support assemblies hold the heat transfer tube.
Both the central hot gas duct and heat transfer tube support
assemblies are hung from the vessel top so that the thermal
expansion is not constrained.

2. Outline of the High-Temperature Continuous Opera-
tion
The high-temperature continuous operation (called HP-
11) started from 17 December 2009 to 21 March 2010.3)
The reactor was started up on 17 December 2009 and shut
down on 27 December 2009 for the purpose of measurement
of control rod worth.
Figure 6 shows the historical trend during the high-
temperature continuous operation. The reactor was started
up on 5 January 2010. The reactor power was raised gradual-
ly and reached to about 98% (29.5 MW) on 22 January 2010.
After the thermal power calibration, the reactor power was
raised to about 99% (29.7 MW) on 31 January 2010. The 50-
day high-temperature continuous operation with full power
was achieved from 22 January 2010 to 13 March 2010.
The reactor power decrease was started on 14 March 2010
and the reactor was shut down on 21 March 2010. The
69 eﬀective full-power operation days were added in the
operation.

(2) IHX Structure Temperature
Figure 11 shows the comparison of the measured temper-
ature with the estimated one at the IHX heat transfer tube
and hot header. The diﬀerence between the estimated tem-
perature and the measured one becomes larger as the tem-
perature increases at the IHX heat transfer tube and the hot
header. Under the condition of IHX inlet coolant temper-
ature over 900C, the estimated temperature at the heat
transfer tube is about 5C higher than the measured one;
this value is about 0.5% of the measured one. The estimated
temperature at the hot header is about 20C lower than the
measured one; this value is about 2% of the measured one.
The temperature diﬀerence is caused by the modeling of
the hot header. The structure near the measurement point of
the hot header is very complex, which is composed of the hot
header and the lower junction tube. The estimation model
does not represent these complex structures in detail.
Thus, the diﬀerence between the measured temperature
and the estimated one can be identiﬁed, and it can be con-
sidered that this estimation method can be applied to predict
the temperature for a vertical helically coiled gas-gas heat
exchanger.

In order to demonstrate the hydrogen production with the
heat generated at the reactor, it is planned that the HTTR is
connected to a hydrogen production system through the IHX
and secondary helium cooling system (called as ‘‘HTTR-
IS’’). The conceptual design of the HTTR-IS system is on-
going in JAEA. It is planned that the temperature of the
secondary helium gas being supplied to the hydrogen pro-
duction system (i.e., secondary helium IHX outlet temper-
ature) is set to about 900C by the reduction of the secondary
helium gas ﬂow rate.
Moreover, in order to make practical use of HTGR, a
future HTGR system ‘GTHTR300C’ producing electric
power and hydrogen is researched and developed in JAEA.
It is planned that a vertical helically-coiled gas-gas heat
exchanger like the IHX is adopted in the GTHTR300C.10)

IV. Conclusions
The 50-day high-temperature continuous operation with
full power was achieved in March 2010 in the HTTR. In this
paper, the heat exchange performance and structure temper-
ature of the IHX were evaluated with the high-temperature
continuous operation data and compared with the designed
one or estimated one.
As a result, the following facts were conﬁrmed:
(1) The IHX can exchange stable high-temperature heat
from the primary coolant to the secondary helium in
the operation.
(2) The structure temperature is under the allowable work-
ing temperature in the operation.
(3) The heat exchange performance is almost the same as
the designed one.
(4) The structure temperature estimated using the analysis
code is almost the same as the measured one; the diﬀer-
ence is about 2%.
The results are very useful for the research and develop-
ment of the HTTR-IS and the future HTGR.

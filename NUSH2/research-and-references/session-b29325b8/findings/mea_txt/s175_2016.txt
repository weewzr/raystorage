In this paper,
 experimental
 works
 involving
 high
 concentration
 CO2 removal
 at elevated pressures
 are
conducted
 using a high pressure
 CO2 pilot plant
 and the result
 is used
 to validate
 a simulation
 model
based
 on established
 models
 in the literature.
 A rate based non-equilibrium
 model
 using 20 wt%
 aque-
ous
 monoethanolamine
 (MEA)
 is developed
 based on the work
 of Pandya
 (1983).
 The model considers
reaction
 kinetics,
 mass transfer rate and heat transfer. Since
 the condition
 of CO2 removal at atmospheric
and
 high pressure
 are different, a pressure modiﬁcation
 index
 is proposed
 and incorporated
 in the mass
transfer
 ﬂux equation
 to account for the non-idealities.
 Comparative
 study
 involving
 the modiﬁed
 model
with
 index-f, original
 rate-based
 non-equilibrium
 model,
 Aspen Plus
 equilibrium
 and non-equilibrium
models
 has also been carried out for the CO2 loading
 at the top column
 exit of 1.505 m. It is found
 that
 the
introduction
 of the proposed
 pressure
 modiﬁcation
 index together
 with proper selection
 of mass
 transfer
and
 effective interfacial
 area correlations
 results
 in an improvement
 in the average
 error from more than
100%
 to as low as 18% between
 the estimated
 and the pilot plant data.

 Introduction
In
 Malaysia,
 CO2 content
 in
 natural
 gas
 ﬁelds
 varies
 from
 28%
 to
87%,
 and
 over
 13
 trillion
 cubic
 feet
 natural
 gas
 reserves
 are
 unde-
veloped
 due
 to
 high
 CO2 content
 (Tan
 et
 al.,
 2012).
 CO2 must
 be
removed
 from
 natural
 gas
 because
 in
 the
 presence
 of
 water
 this
impurity
 can
 form
 acids
 that
 corrode
 pipelines
 and
 other
 equip-
ment.
 Furthermore,
 CO2 provides

 and
 must

removed
 to

 before
 distribution

gas

 The

 CO2 permitted

mitted

 pipeline

 typically

 (Rufford
et

 2012).
 The

 of

 from

 to
be

 cryogenic
 plant

 produce

 recover
 liqueﬁed
petroleum
 gas
 (LPG)
 or
 natural
 gas
 liquids
 (NGL)
 are
 more
 stringent
than

 for
 typical
 gas
 pipelines.
Natural
 gas
 reserves
 have
 high
 ﬂowing
 pressure
 at
 the
 well
head
 that
 come
 from
 the
 reservoir
 at
 a
 pressure
 more
 than
 200
 bar
(Addicks
 et
 al.,
 2002) which
 is
 reduced
 to
 within
 70–100
 bar
 for
subsequent
 operation
 at
 offshore
 platform
 (Tan
 et
 al.,
 2012).

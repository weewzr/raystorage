ABSTRACT
Thermodynamic analysis and optimization for the steam methane reforming (SMR) hydrogen 
production system using the high temperature gas-cooled reactor pebble-bed module power 
plant (HTR-PM) are investigated in this work. Based on the thermodynamic-equilibrium model, 
parameters of thermal efficiency (η), hydrogen production (YH2/methane), methane conversion 
rate (X1) and carbon monoxide conversion rate (X2) are calculated under the specified tem­
perature (T), pressure (P) and water-to-carbon ratio (S). The influence of S, T, P on η, YH2/methane, 
X1 and X2 is then analyzed. It considers a wide range of operating conditions (T = 400–1200°C; 
P = 2–7 MPa and S = 2–10). The results show that the influence of on the system performance is 
significant. When T > 950°C, η and YH2/methane increases slowly (4 < S ≤ 6) or reduces (S > 6). For 
the operating conditions of HTR-PM (P = 7 MPa; S = 6 and T = 950°C), the maximum value of η is 
63.44% and the maximum YH2/methane is 3.3 mol. At last, system optimized parameters are 
illustrated.

1. Introduction
1.1 Nuclear hydrogen production
The main sources of hydrogen are from fossil fuels and 
water. Wind energy, solar energy, and nuclear energy 
are usually considered as the most promising candi­
dates to provide heat for hydrogen production [1]. 
Since the nuclear energy does not generate any green­
house gases and limited by geographical factors [2], 
over the last decades, various hydrogen production 
methods using nuclear energy as heat source have 
been studied and reported.
Among all the hydrogen production methods, ther­
mochemical cycle (I-S cycle [3–7], Cu-Cl [8–13], Mg- 
Cl [14–17] and Co-Cl [18]), Hybrid Sulfur Cycle (HyS 
cycle) [19–22], high-temperature steam electrolysis 
(HTSE) [23–27] and steam methane reforming 
(SMR) [28–31] have attractive prospects. However, 
the thermochemical cycle, such as the HyS cycle and 
HTSE, is either new technology or not particularly 
efficient at hydrogen production [17,22,30,31]. In 
addition, SMR has been extensively applied in various 
industries and accumulated much practical engineer­
ing experience [32–35]. Approximately 96% of the 
hydrogen produced today are extracted from the 
reforming of fossil fuel feedstocks, with SMR 
accounting for 49% [36]. And some studies [37,38] 
reported that the cost of hydrogen production via 
SMR reduces 255 $/ton H2 and decreases 8.8 kg CO2 
/GJ primary energy (natural gas fuel energy), when the 
heat is provided from the nuclear reactors instead of 
the combustion of the methane input. Thus, SMR 
using nuclear reactor as the heat source can produce 
hydrogen from the fully clean energy [39,40].
1.2 Thermodynamic analysis of hydrogen 
production via SMR
Many studies [41–45] about the thermodynamic ana­
lysis of SMR process under different operating condi­
tions have been reported. Tabrizi et al. [46] performed 
a thermodynamic analysis of SMR by combining the 
method of Gibbs free energy minimization (GFEM) 
and statistical modeling under the conditions of the 
water-to-carbon ratio S = 0.5–3, pressure P = 0.1–5 
MPa and reforming temperature T = 600–1200 K. 
Results showed that the temperature plays a crucial 
role in the parameters of methane conversion rate, 
selectivity of gas (H2, CO) and H2/CO ratio. Adiya 
et al. [47,48] studied four ways of SMR (conversional 
steam reforming (C-SR), sorption enhanced steam 
reforming (SE-SR), chemical looping steam reforming

Results show that the increasing of water to carbon 
ratio and temperature have noteworthy influence on 
the methane conversion rate and hydrogen yield when 
T < 1200 K. Carapellucci and Giordano [51] developed 
a thermodynamic equilibrium model and investigated

increases to 3.5 mol/mol CH4.
Since the working pressure P is 7 MPa in the hydro­
gen production system with the High-Temperature 
Gas-Cooled Reactor Pebble-Bed Module power plant 
(HTR-PM), the range of the operating parameters 
(S < 5, P < 3 MPa) investigated in these studies are 
still not enough wide, which cannot be used to evalu­
ate the performances of the SMR hydrogen production 
system using HTR-PM.
1.3 Nuclear hydrogen production using high 
temperature gas cooled reactor
Among the Generation IV nuclear reactors, the high- 
temperature gas cooled reactor (HTGR) including the 
HTR-PM reactor is considered as a favorable heat 
source for hydrogen production via SMR due to its 
high output temperature (750–950°C) [52–54]. Some 
studies [55–58] about the hydrogen production system 
of SMR using HTGR have been performed. The first 
integrated system of the nuclear hydrogen production 
via SMR was experimentally investigated in the ‘EVA 
I and EVA II (Einzelrohr Versuchs Anlage)’ project at 
the Nuclear Research Centre Julich [59–61]. The 
hydrogen production system of SMR connected to 
the HTTR (a HTGR with thermal output of 30 MW), 
was carried out to investigate the system performances 
of nuclear SMR at Japan Atomic Energy Research 
Institute (JAERI) [62–64]. Later, Yin et al. [65,66] 
investigated the thermodynamic analysis of the hydro­
gen production performance using the 10 MW High 
Temperature Gas-cooled Test Reactor (HTR-10). The 
simulation results show that the system performances 
(e.g. hydrogen production rate, thermal efficiency, 
etc.) are remarkably influenced by the inlet helium 
temperature. Chikazawa et al. [67] designed and eval­
uated a hydrogen production plant of steam methane 
reforming with Sodium Cooled Rapid Reactor 
(SCRP). And its economic potential is roughly per­
formed. Wang et al. [39,68] investigated the thermo­
dynamic performances of a coupling system that 
hydrogen 
production 
of 
steam-reforming

high-

Khorasanov et al. [69] investigated the feasibility of 
the nuclear hydrogen production system using BN- 
600 commercial fast reactor (sodium-cooled fast reac­
tor) as heat source in Russia. And its prospects are 
impressive. Ghouse et al. [70] developed and validated 
a rigorous model for the integrated steam reforming 
and nuclear heat system based on first principles. 
Based on the Ghouse’s work, Hoseinzade et al. 
[30,31] developed a dynamic and two-dimensional 
model to design a hydrogen production system via 
SMR 
using 
HTR-Module 
pebble 
bed 
reactor 
(60 MW) as heat source. In the past decades, some 
references [28,71] describing the integrated system of 
the SMR using nuclear reactor as the heat source have 
been reported. However, research with the HTR-PM 
(2x250 MWe) [29,72,73] reactor as the heat source to 
provide heat for SMR process has not been reported. 
Meanwhile, the operational parameters of the conver­
sional SMR that have been reported are not suitable to 
evaluate the performances of the SMR hydrogen pro­
duction system using HTR-PM. Therefore, there is 
a need to carry out the thermodynamic analysis and 
optimization for the SMR hydrogen production sys­
tem using HTR-PM.

To use the High-Temperature Gas-cooled Reactor (HTGR) system as a commercial reactor similar to
the future HTGR ‘GTHTR300C,’ supplying stable high-temperature heat from the reactor to the heat
utilization system such as the hydrogen production system over a prolonged period should be demon-
strated. The 50-day high-temperature continuous operation with full power, which is the ﬁrst long-term
operation with a reactor outlet coolant temperature over 900C, was achieved in March 2010 in the High
Temperature Engineering Test Reactor (HTTR) of the Japan Atomic Energy Agency (JAEA). In order to
supply stable high-temperature heat from the reactor to the heat utilization system using the HTTR, the
heat exchange performance and structure integrity of the intermediate heat exchanger (IHX) should be
conﬁrmed. In this paper, the heat exchange performance and structure temperature of the IHX were
evaluated with the high-temperature continuous operation data and compared with the designed one or
estimated one. As a result, it was conﬁrmed that the IHX can exchange stable high-temperature heat from
the primary coolant to the secondary helium, and the IHX structure temperature is under the allowable
working temperature in the operation. Moreover, it was conﬁrmed that the heat exchange performance is
almost the same as the designed one and that the structure temperature estimated with the analysis code is
almost the same as the measured one.
KEYWORDS: HTGR, HTTR, high-temperature continuous operation, intermediate heat exchang-
er, heat exchange performance, structural integrity
I. Introduction
The
High
Temperature
Engineering
Test
Reactor
(HTTR),1) which is the ﬁrst High-Temperature Gas-cooled
Reactor (HTGR) in Japan, having a thermal power of
30 MW and 950C maximum reactor outlet coolant temper-
ature, was constructed in the Japan Atomic Energy Agency
(JAEA) for the purpose of establishing and improving the
technologies of HTGRs as well as nuclear heat utilization.
The HTTR attained its ﬁrst criticality in November 1998,
and in-service operation has been conducted through ﬁve-
phase rise-to-power tests.
To develop the commercial HTGR system such as the
future HTGR ‘GTHTR300C,’ supplying stable heat to a
heat utilization system over a prolonged period should be
demonstrated. Thus, the long-term continuous operation
with full power and over 900C reactor outlet coolant tem-
perature (called ‘high-temperature continuous operation’)
was planned.
Before the high-temperature continuous operation, the
long-term continuous operation with 850C maximum reac-
tor outlet coolant temperature mode was conducted from
March to April in 20072) in order to verify and conﬁrm the
safety of the high-temperature continuous operation.
The high-temperature continuous operation was started
from January 2010, and the 50-day high-temperature con-
tinuous operation with full power was achieved in March
2010.3) As a result of these operations, it was demonstrated
that the HTTR can supply stable heat to the heat utilization
system such as the thermochemical hydrogen production
system over a prolonged period. These operations are the
ﬁrst achievements in the history of the HTTR. The data
obtained, such as core burnup characteristics, helium purity
management, high-temperature instrument performance, and
core internal structure integrity, are very useful for the
development of a commercial HTGR.
The intermediate heat exchanger (IHX) is one of the high-
temperature instruments installed in the HTTR. It is planned
that the thermochemical hydrogen production system is con-
nected to the HTTR through the IHX, so the IHX is the most
important instrument in the development of a hydrogen
production system and the future HTGR ‘GTHTR300C.’

II. Outline of the HTTR and the High-Temperature
Continuous Operation
1. Outline of the HTTR
(1) Reactor and Fuel
Table 1 shows the major speciﬁcations of the HTTR and
Fig. 1 shows the structure of the reactor of the HTTR. The
reactor is composed of fuel elements, reﬂector block, core
restraint mechanism, reactor pressure vessel (RPV), and so
on. The reactor core is composed of 30 columns of the
fuel blocks and 7 columns of the control rod guide blocks.
The permanent reﬂector blocks and the replaceable reﬂector
blocks are arranged around the reactor core.
Figure 2 shows the components of the fuel element. The
fuel element of the HTTR is the so-called pin-in-block type
composed of fuel rods and a hexagonal graphite block (fuel
block). The fuel block has 31 or 33 holes, and the fuel rods
are inserted in the hole. The primary coolant ﬂows through
the annulus formed between the fuel rod and hole of the fuel
block.
In the reactor, the primary coolant ﬂows in from the
annulus of the primary concentric hot gas duct arranged at
the bottom of the RPV. It rises to the upper plenum through
the gap between the RPV and the permanent reﬂector blocks.
Next, it turns over and ﬂows downward through the fuel
channel. Lastly, heated coolant is joined at the hot plenum
and ﬂows out to the inner pipe of the primary concentric hot
gas duct through the primary coolant outlet nozzle.
(2) HTTR Cooling System
Figure 3 shows the HTTR cooling system. The HTTR
cooling system is composed of the main cooling system
(MCS), the auxiliary cooling system (ACS), and the vessel
cooling system (VCS). The MCS is operated in normal
operation to remove the generated heat at the reactor.
The ACS and VCS are the engineered safety features. The
ACS is in standby during normal operation and starts up to

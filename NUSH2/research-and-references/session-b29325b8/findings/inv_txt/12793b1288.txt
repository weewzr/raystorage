Background
There is an urgent need to control GHG emissions to 
the atmosphere, particularly CO2, which is considered 
to be a major contributor due to its abundance [1,2]. In 
addition to environmental reasons for CO2 removal, 
there are some industrial processes, such as ammo­
nia production and hydrogen production, which also 
require the removal of CO2 [3]. The absorption of CO2 
into chemical solvents (alkanolamines) is one of the 
most promising technologies for capturing CO2 due to 
its maturity, cost–effectiveness and capability of han­
dling large amounts of exhaust stream [3]. One of the 
key parameters for this technology is to use an effec­
tive solvent, which should have fast reaction kinetics, 
high mass-transfer performance, high absorption 
capacity, low energy requirement for regeneration, low 
degradation rate and low corrosiveness.
It is generally accepted that reaction kinetics data are 
one of the most important parameters for CO2 absorp­
tion with chemical solvents. This is because reaction 
kinetics data are required for effectively designing 
and simulating an absorption column [4]. In order to 
obtain reaction kinetics data, CO2 absorption experi­
ments need to be performed using laboratory-scale 
gas–liquid contactors such as stirred cell reactor, wet­
ted wall column, laminar jet absorber, wetted sphere 
absorber, string of disc contactor or stopped-flow appa­
ratus. The absorption rate data obtained from the CO2 
absorption equipment can then be interpreted using 
graphical methods, simplified kinetics models based on 
reaction mechanisms (i.e., zwitterion, termolecular or 
base-catalyzed hydration mechanisms) and compre­
hensive numerically solved reaction kinetics models 
that take into account the coupling between chemical 
equilibrium, mass-transfer and chemical kinetics of all 
possible reactions.
This article is a comprehensive review of the kinet­
ics of CO2 absorption into amine solutions, including 
measurement and determination of kinetics data from 
laboratory-scale gas–liquid contactors, proposed reac­
tion mechanisms for the CO2–amine reaction, proposed 
reaction kinetics models and kinetics behavior.

Reaction kinetics is one of the most important parameters for the amine-based CO2 capture process because 
it indicates how fast CO2 reacts with amine, and it is one of the key parameters required for simulating 
and designing the absorption column. Reaction kinetics has been studied for several decades to aid the 
understanding of the reaction mechanism, obtaining the kinetics data and understanding the kinetics 
behavior. This article will comprehensively review these research activities from past to present and look at 
the future perspective of the study of CO2 absorption kinetics.

Key terms
Kinetics: Study of the rate or speed of 
chemical reaction of a chemical process.
Kinetics models: Mathematical models 
that can be used for extracting kinetics 
data and predicting CO2 absorption rate.
Mechanism: Step or sequence of 
chemical reaction that describes how a 
reaction proceeds.
Gas–liquid mass transfer: Movement of

Rate constant: Parameter that

reaction (the higher the rate constant,

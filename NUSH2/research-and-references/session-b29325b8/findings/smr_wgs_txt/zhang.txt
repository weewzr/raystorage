ABSTRACT
Thermodynamic analysis and optimization for the steam methane reforming (SMR) hydrogen 
production system using the high temperature gas-cooled reactor pebble-bed module power 
plant (HTR-PM) are investigated in this work. Based on the thermodynamic-equilibrium model, 
parameters of thermal efficiency (η), hydrogen production (YH2/methane), methane conversion 
rate (X1) and carbon monoxide conversion rate (X2) are calculated under the specified tem­
perature (T), pressure (P) and water-to-carbon ratio (S). The influence of S, T, P on η, YH2/methane, 
X1 and X2 is then analyzed. It considers a wide range of operating conditions (T = 400–1200°C; 
P = 2–7 MPa and S = 2–10). The results show that the influence of on the system performance is 
significant. When T > 950°C, η and YH2/methane increases slowly (4 < S ≤ 6) or reduces (S > 6). For 
the operating conditions of HTR-PM (P = 7 MPa; S = 6 and T = 950°C), the maximum value of η is 
63.44% and the maximum YH2/methane is 3.3 mol. At last, system optimized parameters are 
illustrated.

1. Introduction
1.1 Nuclear hydrogen production
The main sources of hydrogen are from fossil fuels and 
water. Wind energy, solar energy, and nuclear energy 
are usually considered as the most promising candi­
dates to provide heat for hydrogen production [1]. 
Since the nuclear energy does not generate any green­
house gases and limited by geographical factors [2], 
over the last decades, various hydrogen production 
methods using nuclear energy as heat source have 
been studied and reported.
Among all the hydrogen production methods, ther­
mochemical cycle (I-S cycle [3–7], Cu-Cl [8–13], Mg- 
Cl [14–17] and Co-Cl [18]), Hybrid Sulfur Cycle (HyS 
cycle) [19–22], high-temperature steam electrolysis 
(HTSE) [23–27] and steam methane reforming 
(SMR) [28–31] have attractive prospects. However, 
the thermochemical cycle, such as the HyS cycle and 
HTSE, is either new technology or not particularly 
efficient at hydrogen production [17,22,30,31]. In 
addition, SMR has been extensively applied in various 
industries and accumulated much practical engineer­
ing experience [32–35]. Approximately 96% of the 
hydrogen produced today are extracted from the 
reforming of fossil fuel feedstocks, with SMR 
accounting for 49% [36]. And some studies [37,38] 
reported that the cost of hydrogen production via 
SMR reduces 255 $/ton H2 and decreases 8.8 kg CO2 
/GJ primary energy (natural gas fuel energy), when the 
heat is provided from the nuclear reactors instead of 
the combustion of the methane input. Thus, SMR 
using nuclear reactor as the heat source can produce 
hydrogen from the fully clean energy [39,40].
1.2 Thermodynamic analysis of hydrogen 
production via SMR
Many studies [41–45] about the thermodynamic ana­
lysis of SMR process under different operating condi­
tions have been reported. Tabrizi et al. [46] performed 
a thermodynamic analysis of SMR by combining the 
method of Gibbs free energy minimization (GFEM) 
and statistical modeling under the conditions of the 
water-to-carbon ratio S = 0.5–3, pressure P = 0.1–5 
MPa and reforming temperature T = 600–1200 K. 
Results showed that the temperature plays a crucial 
role in the parameters of methane conversion rate, 
selectivity of gas (H2, CO) and H2/CO ratio. Adiya 
et al. [47,48] studied four ways of SMR (conversional 
steam reforming (C-SR), sorption enhanced steam 
reforming (SE-SR), chemical looping steam reforming

system using HTR-PM.
1.3 Nuclear hydrogen production using high 
temperature gas cooled reactor
Among the Generation IV nuclear reactors, the high- 
temperature gas cooled reactor (HTGR) including the 
HTR-PM reactor is considered as a favorable heat 
source for hydrogen production via SMR due to its 
high output temperature (750–950°C) [52–54]. Some 
studies [55–58] about the hydrogen production system 
of SMR using HTGR have been performed. The first 
integrated system of the nuclear hydrogen production 
via SMR was experimentally investigated in the ‘EVA 
I and EVA II (Einzelrohr Versuchs Anlage)’ project at 
the Nuclear Research Centre Julich [59–61]. The 
hydrogen production system of SMR connected to 
the HTTR (a HTGR with thermal output of 30 MW), 
was carried out to investigate the system performances 
of nuclear SMR at Japan Atomic Energy Research 
Institute (JAERI) [62–64]. Later, Yin et al. [65,66] 
investigated the thermodynamic analysis of the hydro­
gen production performance using the 10 MW High 
Temperature Gas-cooled Test Reactor (HTR-10). The 
simulation results show that the system performances 
(e.g. hydrogen production rate, thermal efficiency, 
etc.) are remarkably influenced by the inlet helium 
temperature. Chikazawa et al. [67] designed and eval­
uated a hydrogen production plant of steam methane 
reforming with Sodium Cooled Rapid Reactor 
(SCRP). And its economic potential is roughly per­
formed. Wang et al. [39,68] investigated the thermo­
dynamic performances of a coupling system that 
hydrogen 
production 
of 
steam-reforming 
for 
methane, 
ethanol 
or 
methanol 
using 
high-

results showed that the reforming temperature playing 
a vital role in the process. And thermal efficiency of the 
coupling system for methane, ethanol and methanol 
was 
61%, 
58.58%

89.17%,

Khorasanov et al. [69] investigated the feasibility of 
the nuclear hydrogen production system using BN- 
600 commercial fast reactor (sodium-cooled fast reac­
tor) as heat source in Russia. And its prospects are 
impressive. Ghouse et al. [70] developed and validated 
a rigorous model for the integrated steam reforming 
and nuclear heat system based on first principles. 
Based on the Ghouse’s work, Hoseinzade et al. 
[30,31] developed a dynamic and two-dimensional 
model to design a hydrogen production system via 
SMR 
using 
HTR-Module 
pebble 
bed 
reactor 
(60 MW) as heat source. In the past decades, some 
references [28,71] describing the integrated system of 
the SMR using nuclear reactor as the heat source have 
been reported. However, research with the HTR-PM 
(2x250 MWe) [29,72,73] reactor as the heat source to 
provide heat for SMR process has not been reported. 
Meanwhile, the operational parameters of the conver­
sional SMR that have been reported are not suitable to 
evaluate the performances of the SMR hydrogen pro­
duction system using HTR-PM. Therefore, there is 
a need to carry out the thermodynamic analysis and 
optimization for the SMR hydrogen production sys­
tem using HTR-PM.

to investigate the thermal efficiency (η), hydrogen 
production (YH2/methane), methane conversion rate

2. Overview of system and model
2.1 SMR hydrogen production system using 
HTR-PM
SMR hydrogen production system using HTR-PM is 
developed to utilize the nuclear heat fully and achieve 
effective thermal efficiency (η) and hydrogen produc­
tion (YH2/methane). The schematic flow diagram of the 
HTR-PM hydrogen production is described in 
Figure 1. Further, a mathematical model to analyze 
the thermodynamic behavior of this hydrogen pro­
duction system is introduced.
Figure 1 shows the process flow chart of SMR 
hydrogen production system using HTR-PM, which 
can supply high-temperature heat of 2x250MWe with 
the temperature of T0 to the intermediate heat exchan­
ger (IHX) in the primary helium gas loop. Then IHX 
transfers the nuclear heat with T1 to the secondary 
helium gas loop that is composed of a helium gas loop, 
a methane feed line and a steam feed line to heat the 
steam reformer (SR) and steam generator (ST), in 
which pressure is regarded as P0 (7 MPa). High- 
temperature helium from IHX first flows to the refor­
mer where the catalytic conversion of methane and 
water vapor generation occurs. During the heat trans­
fer process, the temperature of the inlet helium drops 
from T1 to T2 (750–950°C) due to the loss of heat 
along the pipeline. ST is designed not only to fully 
utilize the residual heat of helium from the reforming 
reactor to supply steam for hydrogen production, but 
also to mitigate the thermal disturbance of the reactor 
and the fluctuation caused by changes of the second­
ary helium gas temperature and system pressure 
(7 MPa).

3. Results and discussion
In this section, the influence of reforming temperature 
(T), water-to-carbon ratio (S) and pressure (P) on 
methane conversion rate (X1) and carbon monoxide 
conversion rate (X2) are investigated according to the 
two reactions of steam-reforming and water gas shift 
as given in Section 2. X1 and X2 also affect the thermal 
efficiency (η). Thus, the influence of X1 and X2 on η are 
also discussed. Further, the variations of thermal effi­
ciency and hydrogen production (YH2/methane) with S, 
T and P are analyzed.
3.1 Analysis of methane and carbon monoxide 
rate conversions
Figure 3. shows the methane conversion rate (X1) 
versus the reforming temperature (T) in the equili­
brium reaction model (Eqs. (11)-(12)) for the pressure 
(P) of 7 MPa at different water-to-carbon ratios (S). X1 
increases rapidly with the increasing T since reaction 
(1) is a strong endothermic reaction. When S > 2, the 
maximum value of X1 is 99.72% in a temperature 
range of 700–1000°C. In addition, it can be seen 
from Figure 3 that increasing S can promote X1 
when P and T are kept the same. This is because that 
increasing S leads reaction (1) forward to the positive 
direction. This relationship that X1 increases with S at 
both the same pressure and temperature, which can be 
seen in Figure 4.
Figure 4 illustrates that methane conversion rate 
(X1) versus steam-to-carbon ratio (S) for the tem­
perature (T) of 850°C at different pressures (P) by 
the equilibrium reaction model. In general, X1 gra­
dually increases with S. When S = 10 and P = 7 MPa, 
X1 reaches 92.1%. Besides, according to the simula­
tion results from Figures 3 and 4, the increment of X1 
decreases as S increases. When S < 6, the increase of 
S by one can increase X1 by 6%-12% for the pressure 
of 7 MPa as well as the temperature in a range of 
700–950°C. When S > 6, increasing S by 1 only 
increases X1 by 1%-3%. When T > 950°C and S > 6, 
there is little impact to increase S on X1. Thus, 
increasing the vapor content of feed gases can appro­
priately improve X1 when T and S are within

Figure 5 presents the carbon-monoxide conversion 
rate (X2) versus reforming temperature (T) at different 
water-to-carbon ratios (S) for the pressure (P) of 7 
MPa. As can be seen from this figure, X2 is about 
100% when T < 500°C. The variations of both T and 
S have no impact on X2. When T > 500°C, X2 decreases 
dramatically as P is 7 MPa. The relationship that the 
increase of T reduces X2 is opposite to the relationship 
of X1 and T (Figure 3). The first reason is that high 
temperature (T > 500°C) accelerates the reactive rate 
of reaction (1), and the second reason is that the water 
gas shift reaction (reaction (2)) is an exothermic pro­
cess. The increase of T inhibits the conversion of CO 
into CO2. However, when the temperature is more 
than 800°C, X2 can be promoted by increasing S. 
Namely, the effect of S on X2 becomes obvious when 
T increases at a certain pressure. Consequently, the 
effect of S should be considered for the SMR hydrogen 
production system using HTR-PM when T > 700°C.
Figure 6 illustrates that the carbon-monoxide con­
version rate (X2) gradually increases with the increase 
of water-to-carbon ratio (S) at different pressures (P) 
when the reforming temperature (T) is at 850°C. 
However, the process of water gas shift has an unfa­
vorable tendency of X2 as T increases. Hence, X2 
cannot be obtained as 100% in the current SMR 
hydrogen production system using HTR-PM. In addi­
tion, P does not affect X2 because of the process of 
equal-volume, which can be seen from Eq. (2). 
Actually, X2 increases slightly with the increase of 
P (Figure 6), which is also contrary to the results as 
observed in Figure 4. The reason is that the reactions 
(1)-(2) is a successive process and the slightly reduc­
tion of hydrogen production promotes X2 as 
P increases. However, the increment of X2 is so small 
that cannot compensate for the decrease of X1 caused 
by the increasing of P. Therefore, SMR hydrogen 
production system using HTR-PM should be operated 
at high temperature and low pressure.

4. Conclusions
In this work, a thermodynamic analysis of methane 
steam reforming using HTR-PM as the heat source has 
been performed. The main conclusions are given as 
below:
Foremost, the temperature T is the most impor­
tant factor that affects the hydrogen production 
performance. Namely, the thermal efficiency η, 
hydrogen production YH2/methane, methane conver­
sion rate X1 are highly related to T. Then, the 
influence of water-to-carbon ratio S on both η and 
YH2/methane is small when the temperature is lower 
than 500°C. And the influence of S on both η and 
YH2/methane become obvious if the temperature is 
larger than 500°C.
Meanwhile, η and YH2/methane increases slowly 
(4 < S ≤ 6) or decreases (S > 6) with T > 950°C. X1 
increases rapidly to its maximum value with the 
increase of T. It is close to 100% (96.83%) when 
T = 1000°C and S = 6. The effect of T on X2 is also 
obvious, while an opposite trend is observed for car­
bon monoxide conversion rate (X2). In addition, 
YH2/methane increases as S increases. In terms of the 
operation parameters (P = 7 MPa, S = 6 and 
T = 950°C) for the SMR hydrogen production system 
using HTR-PM, the maximum value of YH2/methane is 
3.3 mol.
Furthermore, the calculated results show that η 
rapidly increases toward its maximum value with the 
increasing of S and then decreases. In this situation, η 
reaches its maximum value of 63.44% regarding to the 
maximum YH2/methane of 3.3 mol. P does not have 
a significant impact on the system behavior (η, 
YH2/methane, X1) when S is relatively high. Specifically,

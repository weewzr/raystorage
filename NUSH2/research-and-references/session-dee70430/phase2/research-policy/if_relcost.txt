1. Background
The Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/856 of 26 February 2019, defines
relevant costs in Article 5(1), as follows:
"The relevant costs shall be the net extra costs that are borne by the project proponent
as a result of the application of the innovative technology related to the reduction or
avoidance of the greenhouse gas emissions.
The net extra costs shall be calculated as the difference between (i) the best estimate
of economic costs (covering investment and operation) and economic revenues and
operational benefits, and (ii) the best estimate of the economic costs and revenues and
operational benefits of a project using a conventional technology with the same capacity
in terms of effective production of the analogous final product.
The Commission may also decide that the relevant costs shall be the net extra costs,
calculated as the difference between the best estimate of (i) the economic costs
(covering investment and operation) and (ii) the economic revenues and operational
benefits."
The latter methodology described in the article above will be referred to in this
document as the "no reference plant" methodology while the former methodology
(where the calculation includes a comparison with a conventional technology) will be
referred to in this document as the "reference plant" methodology.

2. Relevant costs methodologies

As mentioned in the background section, two relevant costs methodologies exist to
compute the relevant costs of a project which constitute the basis for calculating the
Innovation Fund Grant: the no reference plant (Option 1) and the reference plant
(Option 2) methodologies. The no reference plant methodology is the default option to
use. Only under the specific conditions set out in this document may the reference plant
methodology be used as a fallback option.

These methodologies apply to all topics as defined in the Innovation Fund `regular' calls
for proposals (and do not apply to the Innovation Fund auction calls).

2.1 Description of the methodologies

2.1.1 Option 1 ­ The no reference plant methodology

Option 1 is the default option and corresponds to the provision of the Innovation Fund
Delegated Regulation which defines the relevant costs as "the net extra costs,
calculated as the difference between the best estimate of (i) the economic costs
(covering investment and operation) and (ii) the economic revenues and operational
benefits."

More specifically, the relevant costs shall be calculated using the best estimate of the
undiscounted total eligible project capital expenditure (CAPEX) and the net present
value (NPV)1 of the eligible project revenues, operational benefits, maintenance CAPEX,
and operational costs (OPEX), arising during construction and over the first ten years
of the project's operation. This is the default methodology for all projects.

2.1.2 Option 2 ­ The Reference plant methodology

Option 2 is the-fall back option and corresponds to the provision of the Innovation Fund
Delegated Regulation which defines the relevant costs as "the difference between (i)
the best estimate of economic costs (covering investment and operation) and economic
revenues and operational benefits, and (ii) the best estimate of the economic costs and
revenues and operational benefits of a project using a conventional technology with the
same capacity in terms of effective production of the analogous final product."

This methodology compares the eligible project CAPEX and the NPV of the eligible
project revenues, operational benefits, maintenance CAPEX and OPEX arising during
construction and over the first ten years of operation to those of a reference plant or
unit of the same size and output using a conventional technology.

This fallback methodology must not be used in the case of modification of an existing
plant/unit (for instance building or transforming a part of the existing installation) or
integration of only parts of the equipment, system or process of a new plant/unit. In
this instance, applicants shall use the no reference plant methodology and consider the
incremental step to the existing production process as the Innovation Fund project.

The reference plant methodology may only be used if:

     - The Innovation Fund project relates to the building and operation of a new
          plant/unit.

     - The reference plant/unit has similar characteristics in terms of capacity and
          output as the Innovation Fund project.

1 Net Present Value (NPV) is the difference between the present value (PV) of cash inflows and present value
  of cash outflows with a specific discount rate.

     - The reference plant must be established in an EU/EEA country and complies with
          the EU environmental standards and with EU legislation, including the EU ETS
          benchmark for industrial products where relevant2.

     - Applicants provide the documents necessary to assess the credibility of the
          financial and technical data of the reference plant, including proof of planning of
          such a (reference) plant/unit as an alternative to the project, formal board
          documents, financial reports, internal business plans or studies. These
          documents must include the description of assumptions underlying the financial
          data and calculations (notably for the WACC, price, volumes, inflation rate used

          external market studies if available.

     - A complete and detailed set of verifiable financial projections including CAPEX,
          revenues, operational benefits, maintenance CAPEX, and operational costs
          (OPEX) for the reference plant is added to the applicant's detailed financial
          model.

2.2 Selection of the appropriate relevant costs methodology

In order to harmonize the relevant costs calculation among applicants, the "default"
methodology is Option 1: the no reference plant methodology, that should be suitable
for all projects.

In situations where a reference plant can be defined as described in section 2.1.2 above,
applicants may use Option 2: the reference plant methodology. In this "fall back"
methodology, the project costs and revenues are compared to the CAPEX, OPEX,
maintenance CAPEX and revenues of a plant/unit with conventional technology that
must be established in an EEA country and complying with EU environmental standards
and with EU legislation, including the EU ETS benchmarks for industrial products (where
relevant).

Applicants will need to decide whether or not to apply the default methodology of Option
1. When choosing Option 2, applicants will have to demonstrate that the conditions for
using the reference plant methodology listed in section 2.1.2 apply and ensure the
traceability and transparency of the calculations. Applicants must demonstrate that
they fulfill these conditions and justify the choice of Option 2 in the specific box on cost
efficiency in the Application Form Part B.

If the data is not fully available and/or the estimated values for the reference plant
cannot be justified in full transparency, applicants must use the no reference plant
methodology.

Insufficient demonstration that the conditions for the use of the reference plant
methodology are satisfied will be penalized during the evaluation and may lead to a
score below the threshold for the `quality of the cost efficiency' award criterion.

3. Calculation of relevant costs

3.1 The no reference plant methodology [Option 1]

Under the no reference plant methodology, the relevant costs are calculated as follows:

      Relevant costs = CAPEX + PV of OPEX + PV of maintenance CAPEX ­ PV of
      operational benefits ­ PV of revenues (including during construction where
      applicable), over the first 10 years of operation after entry into operation

Where PV stands for present value; CAPEX, OPEX, maintenance CAPEX, operational
benefits and revenues are defined in the glossary of Appendix 1.

      CAPEX : Project capital expenditure incurred before the entry into operation of the
      project

      PV of OPEX : Present value of project operational expenditure

      PV of maintenance CAPEX : Present value of project maintenance CAPEX incurred
      after the entry into operation of the project

      PV of operational Benefits : Present value of project operational benefits

      PV of revenues : Present value of project revenues

This methodology derives the relevant costs as the best estimate of the total CAPEX
and the NPV of revenues, OPEX, maintenance CAPEX and operational benefits arising
during construction and over the first ten years of operation after the project's
entry into operation3. The PV (and hence the NPV) should be computed using the
project weighted average cost of capital (WACC) described in sections 3.2.1 and 3.4.4
below.

Any additional revenues of the project (for example in the form of monetisation of the
by-products value(s)) or cost savings must also be included in the calculation.

Applicants should pay attention to the parameters described in section 3.4 below. In
particular, non-eligible parameters must be excluded from the relevant costs
calculation. In addition, applicants must justify in detail the scope and computation of
the revenues and costs in their business plan, the applicant's detailed financial model
and application form Part B. All these documents are mandatory and will form an
integral part of the application. Please refer to section 5 of the call document for the
full list of mandatory documents.

3.2 The reference plant methodology [Option 2]

The methodology derives the relevant costs from the difference in CAPEX, net present
value (NPV) of the OPEX, maintenance CAPEX, revenues and operational benefits
arising during construction and over the first ten years of operation after the
project's entry into operation between the project and the reference plant:

      Relevant costs = InnovFund project CAPEX ­ reference plant CAPEX + (PV of
      InnovFund project OPEX and maintenance CAPEX ­ PV of reference plant OPEX and
      maintenance CAPEX) ­ (PV InnovFund project operational benefits ­ PV of

where all elements and systems required for operation of the project have been tested and activities resulting
in effective avoidance of greenhouse gas emissions have commenced. For projects with multiple phases
(meaning several entry into operation dates in connection with implementation of successive phases, for
example for manufacturing projects), the entry into operation is defined as the entry into operation of the
last phase of the project."

      reference plant operational benefits) ­ (PV of InnovFund project revenues ­ PV of
      reference plant revenues)

See glossary in Appendix 1 for the definition of CAPEX, OPEX, maintenance CAPEX,
operational benefits and revenues.

The PV (and hence the NPV) for the reference plant should be computed using a WACC
that will be different than the one for the project (see sections 3.2.1 and 3.4.4 below
for more details).

Any additional revenues of the project (for example in the form of monetisation of the
by-products value(s)) or cost savings also need to be included in the calculation of the
relevant costs.

Applicants should pay attention to the parameters described in section 3.4 below. In
particular, non-eligible parameters must be excluded from the relevant costs
calculation. In addition, applicants must justify in detail the scope and computation of
the revenues and costs in the project's business plan, application form Part B, reference
plant documents, and applicant's detailed financial model, which should include all
computation details for both the project and the reference plant. All these documents
are mandatory and will form an integral part of the application. Please refer to section
5 of the call document for the full list of mandatory documents.

3.2.1 WACC to be used in the Reference plant methodology

In addition to the conditions governing the selection of the reference plant described in
section 2.1.2, applicants need to make specific assumptions with respect to the WACC
for the reference plant to enable a robust calculation of relevant costs under the
reference plant methodology.

WACC for the (innovative) project: Project WACC should follow the guidelines laid down
in section 3.4.4.

WACC for the reference plant: same guidelines as for project WACC except for the
following:

     · for cost of debt, applicants should use levels generally assumed for the sector;
          and

     · for cost of equity, applicants should remove any innovation premium assumed
          in the calculation of the cost of equity for the (proposed innovative) project.

For the leverage in the WACC for the reference plant, applicants should use the same
gearing ratio (D/V and E/V ratios as defined under 3.4.4) as for the project WACC.

3.3 Detailed budget table/relevant costs calculator/financial information file
(FIF) template to calculate relevant costs

The relevant costs are calculated automatically once the appropriate cells in the detailed
budget table/relevant costs calculator (also called `financial information file (FIF)')
relevant costs excel file are filled in.

This template is available via the Funding and Tenders Portal and its use is mandatory
for all applicants. Changes to the formulas or sheets of the FIF are not allowed.

The FIF and the applicant's detailed financial model must include financial projections
over the expected project lifetime, as defined in the glossary of this methodology
and the call text, to ensure that the project's profitability is computed over the whole
expected project lifetime. The project lifetime must not be confused with the project
duration which is also defined in the glossary of the call document. The relevant costs

will be automatically calculated in the FIF on the basis of the total CAPEX, and the NPV
of revenues, OPEX, maintenance CAPEX, and operational benefits arising during
construction and over the first 10 years of operation after entry into operation (including
during construction where applicable). If financial projections are not computed in the
FIF and the applicant's detailed financial model over the expected project lifetime, this
may be penalized during the proposal evaluation under the `financial maturity' award
criterion.

The FIF contains amongst others the following information:

    (1) "Financial Model Summary Sheet" including a summary overview of the financial

           projections including revenues, OPEX and CAPEX, as well as key elements of
           the profit & loss account, balance sheet and cash flow statements. If a project
           anticipates to generate OPEX savings during operation (for example lower fuel
           or heat consumption), these should be appropriately reflected in the projections.

     (2) funding sources and uses table in the "Summary Chart" sheet with the financing
           plan of the project's total CAPEX (eligible and ineligible items) expected at
           financial close

     (3) table in the "Grant Breakdown Inputs" sheet with Innovation Fund grant
           breakdown per work package and beneficiary/affiliated entity

     (4) "RC Calculation" sheet including the details of the relevant costs calculation
           based on the selected relevant costs methodology. Applicants are instructed to
           copy the values of the relevant costs and requested Innovation Fund grant
           amount rounded to the nearest euro as shown at the bottom of the sheet into
           the application form Part A

     (5) cost efficiency calculation in the "Cost Efficiency Calculation" sheet; and

    (6) "Part C Support Sheet" as a support for manually entering the corresponding

           data/indicators in the application form Part C. This sheet is indicative and it is
           the responsibility of the applicant to ensure that the data provided in the Part C
           Support Sheet is accurate and consistent with the other application documents.

The FIF calculates the relevant costs over the first 10 years of the project's operation.
If the entry into operation date does not coincide with the start of a particular calendar
year, the FIF will automatically take the pro rata share of revenues, OPEX, maintenance
CAPEX and operational benefits for the calendar year corresponding to the 10th year of
operation for the calculation of the relevant costs.

3.4.1 General considerations

Boundaries for relevant costs calculation

Applicants must calculate relevant costs in line with the project scope (please refer to
section 2 of the call document for details on activities that can be funded) and the
guidelines in the methodology for the calculation of relevant costs.

All CAPEX, revenues, operating expenditure, maintenance CAPEX and operational
benefits related to investments within the project scope shall be included in the financial
projections. CAPEX and OPEX within the project scope but not eligible for the calculation
of relevant costs must be included in the "Financial Model Summary Inputs" sheet of
the FIF as "CAPEX items not included in relevant costs (if relevant)" or "OPEX items not
included in relevant costs (if relevant)". This is to ensure that these costs are taken
into account in the project's financial projections without impacting the relevant costs
calculation.

The detailed description of which CAPEX and OPEX items are eligible for the calculation
of the relevant costs can be found in the glossary of this document, as well as the
"Intro& Definitions" sheet of the FIF.

The scope of the project must be consistent across the whole application. The same
project scope shall be considered for the description of the `degree of innovation',
`project maturity', `GHG emission avoidance calculation' award criteria, and for the
calculation of the relevant costs, unless otherwise stated in the call document.

Intermediary products

All projects will have an identifiable product or more (final or intermediary) products
which are the output of the project (the valorization of captured CO2 is also an output).
All CAPEX, OPEX, revenues and operational benefits taken into account in the
applicant's detailed financial model and business plan must be linked to this
product/these products. If the product uses intermediary products or goods produced
by installations which are outside of the project scope, then only their purchase price
can be taken into account as project costs.

Incremental / add-on projects

When a project relates to building or transforming part of an existing installation, only
the CAPEX of this new part may be taken into account for the relevant costs
calculations. Likewise, only the contribution of this partial process/installation to the
overall operational cost of the full process/installation may be used in the calculations.
The revenues that can be used are the ones generated by the incremental investment,
not those of the overall existing process. The logic applied for the calculation of relevant

                                                                                                                                                                10
                                                       EU Grants: InnovFund Relevant Costs methodology: V5.0 ­ 01.12.2025

costs must be the same as the one used for the calculation of GHG emissions (i.e.
capturing what has changed with the proposed project).

For example: for a carbon capture project, applicants must use as input for the FIF the
CAPEX of the capture project, its dedicated operational cost (including maintenance
CAPEX as specified above) and the project revenues as well as potential savings related
to the carbon capture project (such as avoided purchase of EU ETS allowances (EUA),
sale of excess of free EUA, and any green premium for the base product of the
existing plant to which the capture unit is added).

Incremental projects may be expected to have low profitability and/or negative
operational cash flows when considered in isolation, but the overall process/installation
with the integrated project included could still be financially viable. Applicants must in
this case add in the applicant's detailed financial model financial projections
demonstrating the aggregated impact of the innovative project on the existing
installation, by inserting separate sheets with the detailed computations.

Integrated projects

In the case of integrated projects (e.g., the off-taker or the feedstock supplier is the
same legal entity or belongs to the same group as the beneficiary producing the
principal/ by-products), the sales price of the production or the purchase price of the
feedstock should be a fair market price, such as the estimated production costs plus a
reasonable margin.

Applicants shall provide a detailed justification for the assumed prices and operating
costs in the business plan. These assumptions must be evidenced through relevant
supporting documents (letters of support, external market studies, past orders, as
appropriate).

Lease financing

Applicants are allowed to include lease expenses (excluding cost of financing the lease)
as OPEX in the relevant costs calculation.

Applicants must clearly identify the owner of an asset that is leased (the lessor) and
submit indicative contract terms to justify the assumed length, schedule and expected
payments of the lease. The purchase option price upon termination of the lease can be
included in the financial projections as OPEX, if there is a reasonable certainty that it
will be exercised and provided that the price is backed by the terms of an indicative
lease contract in the application.

Applicants shall provide the financial projections in connection with the lease contract
over the expected project lifetime to ensure that the project's profitability is computed
over the expected project lifetime. However, for the relevant costs, the FIF will
automatically include only the payments associated with the lease contract in the
calculation that arise within the first ten years of operation after the entry into
operation.

In particular, financial leases are widely used for the financing of newbuilt ships or
planes. The ability of the lessor to reach final investment decision for the constructed
ship or plane and for the lease agreement to be secured by financial close, will be
important aspects for the evaluation of the `financial maturity' award criterion.

Leasing of existing producing assets/facilities commissioned and under operation before
submission of the application (such as existing manufacturing plants or energy
generation assets) is not permitted (in line with provisions under "Other excluded
items" in section 3.4.9).

Contingencies

Applicants are allowed to include contingencies in their CAPEX. However, robust
justification must be included in the business plan as to why these contingencies are
required, why they are at the level proposed. For example, this justification can be
presented in the form of a deterministic calculation supported by a benchmark or
previous experience which must be referenced, or a probabilistic calculation which must
be accompanied by a summary of the calculation method. The soundness of this
justification will be taken into account in the evaluation of the project's financial
maturity.

3.4.2 Maritime and Aviation transport sectors

Installation of innovative technology into a new or retrofitted ship/plane

Eligible activities for maritime and aviation projects are described in the call text.

The examples below provide guidance for projects related to the production and/or
installation of innovative technology (e.g. energy system, engine or equipment) into a
new or retrofitted ship or plane.

Example 1: manufacturing and installation of an energy-efficient engine on a newbuild
vessel where construction of the newbuild vessel falls outside the project's scope

Applicants must provide sufficiently detailed information in their application (for
example in the form of indicative contracts/heads of terms) to justify the scope and
estimated costs related to the part of the innovative technology which is built and
installed on the vessel.

For the relevant costs calculation, applicants shall use the no reference plant

methodology and consider as the Innovation Fund project the incremental CAPEX,
revenue and OPEX associated with the installation of the new equipment/system
technology on the newbuilt ship.

The CAPEX, OPEX, savings and revenues for the relevant costs calculation shall remain
within the following boundaries: the CAPEX of producing and installing the innovative
technology; the OPEX for operating the innovative technology; and revenues and
savings proven to be directly derived solely from the new technology.

In this specific example, applicants will use as input for the FIF: the CAPEX for the
engineering, manufacturing and installation of the engine (where relevant), the OPEX
linked to the engine's operation (for example fuel costs and maintenance service costs),
and revenues directly related to the engine installation (such as avoided purchase of
EU ETS allowances or the potential green premium paid by customers for operating the
vessel with the new technology). However, in this specific example, any CAPEX related
to the construction of the newbuild vessel, or revenues (such as charter income), or
OPEX (for example crewing, insurance, port or dry docking costs) from operation of the
vessel with no direct link to the operation of the engine must be excluded from the
relevant costs calculation since they are falling outside of the project scope.

Example 2: construction of a manufacturing plant of engines for small and medium-
sized vessels

For the relevant costs calculation, the project scope is limited to the manufacturing of
engines.

In this specific example, applicants will use as input for the FIF: the CAPEX associated
with the plant construction and equipment; Revenues from the sale of engines to

and labor costs.

3.4.3 Financial projections

Key data inputs are based on financial projections over the expected project lifetime,
which applicants are required to provide when filling in the FIF.

In addition, applicants are required to provide the applicant's detailed financial model
(i.e. mandatory as defined in section 5 of the call document). This model should include
all detailed financial assumptions - derived among others from feasibility studies,
preliminary contract terms, market analysis and other evidence based on the status of
the project to date - as well as project financial statements (i.e. cash flow, profit and
loss and balance sheet statements for the project), return computation, and sensitivity
analysis. The detailed model should use formulas instead of hard figures and no macros.
The projections of the applicant's detailed financial model must be consistent with those
used in the FIF to derive the calculation of relevant costs. For this purpose, applicants
can add and merge sheets of the detailed financial model and the FIF. Applicants
must still submit the FIF and the applicant's detailed financial model as two
distinct mandatory documents.

If the applicants chose the reference plant methodology, they should add in the
applicant's detailed financial model the same type of information for the reference plant
as for the innovative project, by inserting separate sheets with the detailed
computations.

The financing plan must cover all the project costs. Non-eligible CAPEX and OPEX, which
are excluded from the relevant costs calculation, should be clearly identified and
encoded in the appropriate cells in the FIF4.

Expansion CAPEX incurred after the defined date for entry into operation must not be
included in the financial projections of the FIF, as they are considered outside the scope
of the Innovation Fund project.

The details of which elements of CAPEX are eligible for the calculation of the relevant
costs can be found in the glossary of this document and in the sheet "Intro & definitions"
of the FIF.

Applicants must separate maintenance CAPEX from CAPEX in the applicant's detailed
financial model. For the purposes of the relevant costs calculation, maintenance CAPEX
must be solely related to maintaining the project's operational capacity and can only
be incurred after the project's entry into operation and are discounted, alongside OPEX.

Costs for activities incurred before the first day of the month after the proposal
submission date are not eligible for the calculation of the relevant costs (please refer
to section 10 of the call document). Therefore, any expenses incurred before the first
day of the month following the proposal submission date, must be included in the
"Financial Model Summary Inputs" sheet of the FIF as "CAPEX items not included in the
relevant costs (if relevant)". This is to ensure that costs incurred before the proposal's

3.4.4 Project WACC

The minimum required project return from investors is represented through the
weighted average cost of capital (WACC) of the Project. This is the blended cost of
capital of the project depending on the ratio of equity and debt expected to be secured
at financial close to finance the project CAPEX. It is calculated using the following
formula and the parameters detailed in Table 1 below:

WACC = E/V * Re + D/V*Rd * (1-Td)5

     · Re = cost of equity

     · Rd = cost of debt

     · E/V = equity portion of total capital (equity over total value of capital) to fund
          project CAPEX

     · D/V = debt portion of total capital (debt over total value of capital) to fund
          project CAPEX

     · Td = marginal tax rate prevailing in the country of the project

The WACC is applied to discount future revenue and cost streams over the expected
project lifetime. The WACC is an important mechanism to help reflect overall project
risks. The WACC level also underpins the assessment of project profitability by external
evaluators and must therefore be realistic. For example, using a very low WACC level
- more in line with a company WACC - for a high-risk project is likely to be challenged
by evaluators.

To ensure equal treatment between projects, applicants must follow the methodology
provided in this section to derive the project WACC and are not allowed to use their

3.4.5 Carbon price and EU ETS allowances

Projects in sectors regulated by the EU ETS Directive that reduce their GHG emissions
will, in most cases, benefit from revenues from the sale of the free allocation of EU ETS
allowances that they will receive and do not need to surrender because of the reduced
emissions below the applicable benchmark(s). Revenues from the sale of excess CO2

emission allowances need to be included in the "Financial Model Summary Inputs" sheet

of the FIF and will be treated as operational benefits in the relevant costs calculation.

Applicants should assume carbon prices as they consider appropriate over the expected
project lifetime, explaining why they have chosen to follow this approach in the
application form and/or business plan. The carbon price projected over time must be
consistently stated in the FIF.

While installations could theoretically hold onto the excess allowances for sale later, for
the purposes of the relevant costs calculation, the excess allowances are assumed to
be sold in the year received. Similarly, applicants should include as OPEX savings of
the project any savings made from the need to buy a smaller amount of EU ETS
allowances due to the lower emissions of the proposed project.

If, in addition, applicants are expecting to generate revenues from the sale of CO2 as
a final product or by-product for commercial use, irrespective of whether this will lead
to saved EU ETS allowances or not, these revenues should also be included as
operational benefits.

Finally, if applicants are expecting to generate revenues from the sale of carbon
removal certificates, these revenues should also be included as operational benefits.

3.4.6 Indexation/inflation

Indexation refers to the adjustment of OPEX, CAPEX and sale prices to the forecast
inflation over the expected project lifetime. Applicants are free to provide their best
estimates of indexed OPEX, CAPEX and sales prices, including inflation, with a clear
breakdown of the different components and justification for the assumptions used for
inflation. All values in the financial projections must be in nominal terms.

3.4.7 By-products and savings

If the project anticipates to generate additional revenues by selling by-products (such
as heat or biochar) or services (for instance, waste gate fees) derived from the
innovative production process, the corresponding value generated should be considered
as a revenue from by-products in the "Financial Model Summary Inputs" sheet of the
FIF.

If the production process will reduce costs through more efficient use of energy/heat
for example, the corresponding value of cost savings should be encoded as "OPEX
savings" in the "Financial Model Summary Inputs" sheet of the FIF and will have the
effect of reducing OPEX in the relevant costs calculation.

3.4.8 Decommissioning

If decommissioning costs of the assets of the project arise during the first 10-year
period, they may be taken into account as part of the relevant costs calculation. Cost
estimates will vary from project to project and therefore need to be accurately
accounted for in the calculation. Applicants will have to justify both the presence and
the amount of these costs. Any revenue resulting from the sale of decommissioned
assets arising during the first 10 years of operation after entry into operation shall also
be included in the relevant costs calculation.

3.4.9 Other public support

Additional 8 public support can take the form of support from other EU funding
programmes or state aid9.

When state aid or other EU funding, in connection with costs incurred after the first day
of the month following the proposal submission date, is secured, has been requested
or is considered to be part of the financing plan, and this additional public support is
necessary to proceed with the final investment decision (FID) for the full investment
envisaged within the project scope, applicants must include the state aid or other EU
funding in their financial projections and provide any information and evidence available
to demonstrate this public support was secured or is in the process of being secured,
to convince evaluators of the likelihood of obtaining this additional public support. The
risk analysis should also include credible mitigation measures such as back-up financing
to close the financing plan if this additional public support is not confirmed before
financial close.

If applicants consider that their financing plan (and FID) does not rely on state aid or
any other EU funds, that could be requested in the future, then this additional public
support does not necessarily have to be included in the FIF and the applicant's detailed
financial model.

                document, section 9) and excluded from the relevant costs calculation.

          B. As opposed to this, any other type of other EU support or state aid, such as
                fiscal or parafiscal measures, tariff reductions resulting in lower operating

3.4.10 Parameters excluded from relevant costs calculation

The two relevant costs methodologies derive the relevant costs from the best estimate
of the CAPEX and the net present value of revenues, OPEX, maintenance CAPEX and
operational benefits arising during construction and over the first ten years of
operation. These terms are defined in the glossary of this guidance.

Any other components should be excluded from the relevant costs calculation.

Examples of such excluded components are presented in the list below. This list of
examples should be considered as non-exhaustive.

     - Terminal value

          Terminal value of assets must not be taken into account in the relevant costs
          calculation. The exclusion of terminal value is consistent with the fact that the
          project IRR is computed over the expected project lifetime.

     - Write-off of existing (old) assets or technologies

          It is recognized that some applicants may have to replace old technology that is
          not fully depreciated. For the purposes of calculating the relevant costs and
          regardless of the relevant costs methodology selected, any write-off cost

                                                                                                                                                                18
                                                EU Grants: InnovFund Relevant Costs methodology: V5.0 ­ 01.12.2025

     associated with the replacement of existing technologies should be excluded.
     Similarly, costs linked to accelerated depreciation of stranded assets (e.g.
     depleted fields), decommissioning of existing assets, reallocation of existing
     buildings, cleaning of used sites to be used for the project, are not eligible.
- Costs incurred before submission of the application
     As stated in section 3.4.3, costs for activities incurred before the first day of the
     month after the proposal submission date are not eligible for the calculation of
     the relevant costs (please refer to section 10 of the Call document). Any
     expenses incurred before the first day of the month following the proposal
     submission date must be included in the "Financial Model Summary Inputs"
     sheet of the FIF as "CAPEX items not included in the relevant costs (if relevant)"
     to ensure that they are not included in the relevant costs calculation. Costs are
     considered as incurred on a certain date if on that date they have already been
     paid or if the obligation to pay has been established on or before that date.
     In other words, costs linked to a contract concluded before the proposal
     submission date can be considered eligible for the relevant costs computation if
     all the following conditions are met:

           - they are linked to services to be performed /equipment delivered after
                 that date

           - they relate to invoices issued after that date, and
           - the corresponding payments are made after that date.
- Debt financing-related Costs
     Costs linked to the debt financing of the project are not eligible and must be
     excluded from the relevant costs computation. A non-exhaustive list of such
     costs is the following: interest during construction, working capital needs, bank
     fees, legal fees, upfront fees, commitment fees, interest payments, additions to
     the maintenance reserve account or debt service reserve account.
- Other excluded items
     VAT, taxes, royalties/license fees paid to project shareholders, management
     fees, acquisition of producing assets/facilities commissioned and under
     operation before submission of the application, communication and marketing
     costs of other products, or reorganization costs are also excluded from the
     relevant costs computation. In-kind contributions are not eligible for the purpose
     of the relevant costs calculations.

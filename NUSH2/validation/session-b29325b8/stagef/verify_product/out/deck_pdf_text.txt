What are High-Temperature Gas-Cooled Reactors?
□High temperature gas-cooled reactors (HTGRs) are advanced 
reactors that used helium, an inert gas, instead of water as coolant.
□Allows higher temperature (~750°C – 1000°C)
□higher efficiency in electricity production
□allows other applications, e.g., hydrogen production, 
petrochemical processing, etc.
□Uses TRISO fuel – a vastly improved fuel – in spherical balls or 
prismatic blocks
□The fuel is embedded into 100,000s of billiard ball-sized “pebbles” or 
prismatic blocks of graphite.
□Nuclear-grade graphite is used as neutron moderator (reduce speed 
of neutrons), structural materials as well as reflector of neutrons.

What are TRISO particles?
□Each TRISO particle consists of a kernel of uranium dioxide 
(UO₂) fuel (sometimes UC or UCO), which has been coated

□dense outer layer of pyrolytic carbon (OPyC)

□Pebbles move downwards due to gravity. They are discharged at the

How would the enhanced safety of small modular reactors affect siting?
□Conventional large reactors requires a large “emergency planning zone (EPZ)” of around 15 – 30 km to ensure that population around the 
plant can be protected or evacuated in the unlikely event of a severe accident with large release of radioactive materials.
□For small modular reactors (including HTGR-type), due to the (1) 
smaller core radionuclide inventories and (2) efficient passive or 
“inherent” safety systems, EPZ are expected to be substantially smaller.
□Small modular reactor vendors target to reduce to hundreds of metres 
or within plant site boundary – anyone outside EPZ will not be 
subjected to dose above regulatory dose limits.
□This could allow small modular reactors to be located closer to 
population centres, or to co-locate with industrial facilities or possibly 
replace current coal plants.

===== page 66
Will small modular reactors be too costly (per kWh) to be used?
There are only a couple of real FOAK small modular reactors and no NOAK small modular reactors at this time to give a good real world 
comparison of the cost (say LCOE) of small modular reactors vs large conventional reactors. Following are points for consideration:
□All first-of-a-kind (FOAK) reactors are very costly due to the development cost, new supply chain, regulatory cost, etc. Cost is 
expected to fall significantly for the Nth-of-a-kind (NOAK) reactor as seen in various past examples in most countries.
□Further cost savings could come from manufacturing (in a factory) vs construction (at the site) due to the modular nature of small 
modular reactors.
□Additional cost to use conventional large reactors, e.g., grid improvement, will have to be taken into account in comparison, or for 
isolated communities that are not connected to grid.
□Small modular reactors built for special purpose, e.g., high temperature heat for petrochemical industry or thermochemical 
hydrogen production, may also be more cost effective.
□Cost of the nuclear island is usually a small part (typically < 20% in US) of the total cost of a nuclear power construction which 
includes the balance of plant (turbine, electrical yards, EPC costs, etc.).
□With expected shorter construction time and flexibility in deployment for small modular reactors, financing could be significantly 
easier with lower cost.
